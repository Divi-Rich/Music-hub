Lonely Streets

⏱ 2 min read

By Asake Jr

Opening line:

"Walking down these lonely streets tonight..."

About the Song

"Lonely Streets" is a reflective track about pushing through hard times with faith that things will get better. The song leans on a familiar Naija songwriting theme: a mother's encouragement as an anchor during uncertain seasons. Asake Jr uses the imagery of empty streets at night to represent isolation and self-doubt, while the hook carries a message of quiet resilience — the idea that staying consistent eventually pays off, even when progress feels invisible.

The Nights

⏱ 2 min read

By Avicii

Opening line:

"He said, one day you'll leave this world behind..."

About the Song

"The Nights" is one of Avicii's most enduring records, released in 2014 as part of a short film series produced with a beverage brand. The song is built around a father's advice to live boldly and make memories worth telling. Avicii, the Swedish producer behind global hits, wrote much of his music around themes of legacy and mortality, which became especially poignant following his passing in 2018. The track remains a favorite at celebrations and farewells for its uplifting, anthemic feel.

Billionaire

⏱ 2 min read

By Teni

Opening line:

"Otedola o l'ori meji, Dangote o l'ori meji..."

About the Song

"Billionaire" is a high-energy anthem from Nigerian singer Teni, built around ambition and the hustle mentality common in Naija pop. The opening line references Femi Otedola and Aliko Dangote — two of Nigeria's most prominent billionaires — as aspirational figures, a common device in Afrobeats where real-life business icons are name-dropped to symbolize success. The track's playful, chant-like hook made it a favorite at parties and a staple on Naija playlists after its release.

By Rema

A smooth, melodic love song built on gentle percussion and a catchy, repeatable hook.

"Calm Down" became one of the most successful Afrobeats exports in history, spending over a year on the Billboard Hot 100 after a remix with Selena Gomez. Rema's melodic, Afro-fusion style — sometimes called "Afrorave" — blends Nigerian rhythms with global pop sensibilities, which helped the track cross over into mainstream markets far beyond Nigeria.

Way Maker

⏱ 2 min read

By Sinach

Opening line:

"You are here, moving in our midst..."

About the Song

"Way Maker" is arguably the most globally influential Nigerian gospel song of the past decade. Written and performed by Sinach, a Lagos-based worship leader and songwriter, the track has been covered by artists worldwide including Michael W. Smith and Leeland, and translated into multiple languages for churches across continents. Its simple, repeating declarations — describing God as a Way Maker, Miracle Worker, Promise Keeper — are designed for congregational worship, which is part of why it spread so widely beyond Nigeria.

Nara

⏱ 2 min read

By Ada Ehi

Vibe:

An energetic praise anthem sung largely in Igbo, built for dancing in celebration.

About the Song

Ada Ehi is known for blending contemporary gospel production with Nigerian indigenous languages, and "Nara" — meaning roughly "receive" or "take" in Igbo — follows that pattern. The song is built for high-energy praise and dance, a style distinct from the slower worship ballads that dominate much of the genre, showing the range within Nigerian gospel music.
